How the transformation works

  1. Reference. You provide a photo (or use a sample). It is downscaled in-browser; nothing is uploaded anywhere.
  2. Extraction. An on-device pose model finds 33 body joints, and a segmentation model cuts the person out of the background. You can see both in the “Extracted person” panel.
  3. 3D body volumes. Each body part becomes a volumetric capsule/ellipsoid. Its width is measured from your silhouette; its depth is procedurally inferred (elliptical cross-sections, ~0.8× the width). Joint depth hints come from the pose model.
  4. Particles. Up to 40,000 particles are sampled through the volume — front and back — and colored from the photo. The hidden back side deterministically reuses mirrored front colors, darkened for a volume cue. Same seed + same photo ⇒ identical character.
  5. Rig & play. Particles are bound to a 28-joint skeleton with forward kinematics, so sliders and animations deform the whole volume coherently from any camera angle.

Limitations (by design)

  • The back of the person is hallucinated, not reconstructed — rotate behind and you see a plausible guess.
  • Loose clothing, occlusions and cropped bodies degrade gracefully: missing parts are skipped, never invented as fact.
  • Joint rotations are world-axis approximations — great for play, not a biomechanics lab.
  • If the ML models can’t load (offline), the app falls back to a procedural body fitted to your photo’s framing.

Tips

Full-body · facing the camera · arms slightly away from the torso · plain background. Try the Depth color mode while orbiting to feel the volume.
